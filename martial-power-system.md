# Martial Power System

**Status:** Core concept in development. The broad structure is established; the exact mechanics of the upper levels remain open.

The scale is a fictional world-building framework. Its inspirations are reference points, not rules copied directly from another setting or real-world belief system.

## 1. The Level 1–9 framework

The world uses a broad martial power scale from **Level 1 to Level 9**.

The levels are narrative benchmarks, not precise mathematical measurements. They describe broad differences in overall capability and consistency. They do not calculate a guaranteed winner.

### Levels 1–7: the human range

The concept of seven levels is inspired by the idea of **Kundalini and the seven chakra centres**.

In this setting, **Level 7 is the natural human ceiling**.

A human may become extraordinarily skilled, disciplined and powerful within the normal limits of the world, but simply training harder does not automatically allow them to pass Level 7. Level 7 represents the highest level of martial achievement normally possible for a human being.

### Level 8 and Level 9: beyond the normal human ceiling

Levels 8 and 9 are inspired by ideas discussed by Kim Goguen and are being adapted into fictional world-building concepts.

They represent territory beyond the ordinary human range. Crossing the Level 7 ceiling requires an exceptional cause: something other than ordinary training must have happened.

The exact mechanisms have not yet been defined. Possible categories include an external source of power, an altered state of body or spirit, a rare substance or artefact, or a transformation with a significant cost. These are possibilities, not fixed rules.

**Level 9 remains undefined.** It should not be treated as an ordinary next step or used to solve a story problem until its nature is deliberately established.

## 2. A level is not a guaranteed victory

A level indicates broad capability; it does not determine every fight.

- Even a Level 1 fighter can produce something meaningful. Level 1 does not mean useless or incapable.
- A lower-level fighter can defeat someone at Level 2 or 3 by controlling the conditions and making the opponent play their game.
- A specialist might demand a kick-only contest because that favours their own strongest skill and removes some of the opponent's options.
- Style, tactics, terrain, rules, weapons, numbers, surprise, fatigue, injury and the objective of the encounter can all change the outcome.
- Winning one encounter does not prove that the winner has greater overall ability.

The distinction is between **overall level** and **match-up advantage**. A Level 3 fighter may be more capable overall than a Level 1 fighter, while still losing a particular contest because the Level 1 fighter has shaped it to favour their speciality.

The levels should help readers understand the scale of capability without turning every fight into a simple comparison of numbers.

## 3. Illustrative reference points

These are the creator's personal artistic analogies for communicating the broad progression of Levels 1–7. They are subjective reference points, not objective rankings of the artists or a judgement of the value of their work.

| Level | Artistic analogy |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hajime Isayama |
| 2 | Tatsuki Fujimoto |
| 3 | Hui King Sum |
| 4 | Masashi Kishimoto |
| 5 | Ma Wing Sing; Khoo Fuk-lung |
| 6 | Yang Kyung-il; Hiroaki Samura |
| 7 | Takehiko Inoue |

The point of the analogy is that even the first level can create something worthwhile. Higher levels represent increasingly exceptional ability, not a division between people who can create and people who cannot.

## 4. Level 7: the human pinnacle

The creator's martial-arts reference for Level 7 is **楊過 (Yang Guo)** at an imagined peak combining the mastery associated with **九陰真經** and **九陽神功**.

The important idea is not a literal reproduction of another novel's techniques. It is the image of a fighter who has reached an extraordinary synthesis of martial knowledge, internal power, technique, experience and combat ability.

A Level 7 fighter is terrifyingly capable, but still belongs to the human martial world. They can be challenged, outmanoeuvred, injured, exhausted or defeated. Different Level 7 fighters may have very different strengths and weaknesses.

Level 7 is the ceiling, not invincibility.

## 5. Level 8: a qualitative break, not bigger explosions

The creator's reference for Level 8 is **東方不敗 (Asia the Invincible)** as portrayed in *Swordsman II* (1992), particularly the way the character plays with opponents, including without even needing to look at them.

The frightening feature is not destructive scale. It is the difference in control and capability: ordinary martial combat no longer presents a meaningful challenge in the way it does to human fighters. Even a Level 7 opponent may seem to belong to a completely different order.

Level 8 does **not** mean blowing up mountains, splitting the moon, or producing ever-larger explosions. It is about the gulf in capability, not the size of the spectacle.

A true Level 8 should be extraordinarily rare and remain far beyond ordinary human progression. It should feel almost off-limits, not like the next rank that talented fighters can expect to reach.

## 6. Crossing the ceiling needs a reason

The governing rule is:

> **Level 7 is the human ceiling. Level 8 requires an exceptional cause; it is not achieved simply by training harder.**

Exceptional power should have consequences, limitations, or a cost. The existence of a rare Level 8 should not make Level 8 the new normal.

A further distinction is useful: a temporary burst with output comparable to Level 8 is not automatically the same as being a true Level 8. A borrowed or external power source might let someone produce an extraordinary effect for a moment without giving them the sustained control, consistency or effortless command that defines a genuine Level 8.

## 7. The Right Hand and the royal elixir

The story of **The Two Hands** is the first major test of this rule.

The Right Hand's natural ceiling is around Level 5–6. He cannot train his way into permanent Level 8 status.

A stolen royal elixir temporarily gives him access to extraordinary bursts with power comparable to Level 8. This is not the same as becoming a true Level 8 martial artist. The borrowed power is limited and eventually exhausted; his underlying ability, judgement and tactical vulnerabilities remain relevant.

This distinction allows the Right Hand to display frightening power without invalidating the human ceiling or making him invincible.

## 8. Current broad reference points

- **Level 1–3:** Developing or capable martial artists. Individual style, tactics and match-ups can matter enormously.
- **Level 4–5:** Highly accomplished fighters; Level 5 is broadly comparable to warlord level.
- **Level 6:** Elite masters.
- **Level 7:** The highest normally achievable human level; the pinnacle of known martial achievement and top S-class territory.
- **Level 8:** An exceptionally rare state beyond the human ceiling, requiring an extraordinary cause.
- **Level 9:** Undefined. Do not use it as an escalation device.

These descriptions are broad guideposts, not rigid job titles or automatic outcomes in combat.

## Open questions

- What exactly distinguishes a Level 7 from a Level 6 in overall capability?
- How much variation can exist between two Level 7 fighters?
- What, precisely, makes a true Level 8 different from a temporary Level 8-like burst?
- What kinds of events can allow someone to cross into Level 8, and what costs or limitations follow?
- What is Level 9, if anything, and should it be reachable at all?
- How should the seven chakra-inspired stages be adapted into the world's own mythology without simply copying real-world beliefs?
