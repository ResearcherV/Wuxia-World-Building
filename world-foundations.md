# World Foundations

This is the first organised record of the setting. It is intentionally provisional: the archive should grow from conversation, not force every idea into a rigid structure too early.

## 1. The world exists beyond its protagonists

**Established foundation**

The setting is not built around one permanent main character. It should work more like a continuing world in which many stories can happen.

A protagonist may be absent for an entire story. A minor character may become the lead in another. People can die, disappear, return, become legends, or fade into the background. The world carries on.

This allows different creators to tell stories about very different people and situations without needing every story to be about the strongest fighter or the same central hero.

## 2. The Seven Kingdoms

**Established foundation**

The world contains seven major kingdoms. They counterbalance one another rather than one kingdom simply dominating the entire setting.

**Working proposal**

The balance need not be perfectly equal. One kingdom might have greater wealth, another a stronger army, another superior trade routes, and another more influential martial organisations. Their strengths and weaknesses create political dependencies and friction.

**Open questions**

- Are the seven kingdoms neighbouring states, or are some separated by seas, mountains, deserts, or other barriers?
- Is there a shared historical origin, or did they develop independently?
- What prevents one kingdom from permanently conquering the others?

## 3. Authority is not the same as a crown

**Established foundation**

A king does not necessarily exercise all the power of the state. A ruler may function mainly as a diplomatic representative or figurehead while ministers, military leaders, powerful families, regional lords, or other institutions hold practical authority.

The balance can differ from kingdom to kingdom. Some regional lords may be closely aligned with the central authority; others may be difficult to control.

**Working proposal**

The political reality of a kingdom can be understood through several overlapping powers: the throne, court officials, regional lords, military command, wealthy interests, and recognised martial organisations.

## 4. The Jianghu is part of society

**Established foundation**

Martial artists are not detached from ordinary life. They need food, shelter, equipment, medicine, transport, information, and money. Clans need income to maintain their members and estates. Gangs and other organisations also need businesses or revenue.

Organisations exist beyond the direct control of the imperial or central government. They may cooperate with the authorities, oppose them, compete with one another, or simply pursue their own interests.

## 5. Power has many forms

**Established foundation**

Martial strength is only one kind of power. Political influence, wealth, resources, information, reputation, skilled personnel, trade networks, territory, and alliances can all matter.

A powerful fighter is not automatically a capable administrator, merchant, strategist, doctor, or politician.

**Working proposal**

A fight can be won while the larger conflict is lost. An organisation might defeat a rival in combat but lose its trade routes, customers, political protection, or income.

## 6. Conflict does not always mean killing

**Established foundation**

Winning and losing do not have to mean life and death. Rivalries can be settled, paused, or redirected through business competition, negotiation, political manoeuvring, reputation, territorial influence, alliances, or financial pressure.

This creates room for ongoing relationships and conflicts rather than making every disagreement an irreversible blood feud.

## 7. Recognised organisations and the state

**Established foundation**

Some clans or organisations may be recognised by the imperial authorities. Recognition can come with partial funding, privileges, or official standing. In return, the organisation may be expected to provide assistance when and where it is needed.

**Working proposal**

Such organisations can also become useful allies or sources of support in internal political struggles. Their loyalty may be valuable, but it should not be assumed to be unconditional.

## 8. A competitive ecosystem

**Established foundation**

There can be many organisations competing in the same field. No single guild, clan, or company needs to monopolise an entire industry.

The comparison raised during discussion was the variety of competing supermarkets in the UK: several businesses can serve the same broad market while differing in reach, reputation, quality, price, and influence.

**Possible examples — not yet fixed canon**

- Blacksmith clans or workshops competing over weapon quality and custom work.
- Medical associations, schools, family practices, and travelling healers.
- Mercenary organisations and job-listing networks operating across kingdoms.
- Delivery, courier, and transport businesses.
- Intelligence and information networks that gather or trade knowledge across regions.

## 9. Gangs and the growth of unofficial authority

**Established foundation**

A gang can become something like the next lord in the making if the actual lord is careless. It may grow from controlling a small criminal or commercial niche into exercising practical authority over a territory.

**Working proposal: stages of growth**

1. It establishes a foothold in activities such as gambling, entertainment, protection, smuggling, or local businesses.
2. It builds relationships with residents, traders, informants, and influential figures.
3. People begin turning to the gang to settle disputes or solve problems instead of approaching the lord's officials.
4. It develops reliable income, fighters, information, and local influence.
5. It becomes a parallel authority — perhaps eventually more effective or more trusted locally than the official administration.

The government or lord might attempt to destroy it, negotiate with it, recognise or absorb it, or ignore it until it becomes too powerful.

**Suggested term: Shadow Lords**

A possible name for people who hold the practical power of a lord without possessing the official title.

Suggested saying: *“A lord rules by decree. A shadow lord rules because everyone already listens.”*

Both the term and saying are proposals, not settled terminology.

## 10. Martial power must have limits

**Established foundation**

The setting should avoid uncontrolled power escalation. A character does not need to become stronger than every previous character, and a new story does not need a bigger threat simply because an earlier story had a powerful fighter.

A level is a broad indication of a fighter's overall capability, not a guaranteed result in every encounter. A lower-level fighter can still win by using a favourable style, choosing the conditions, exploiting a weakness, changing the rules of engagement, or making the higher-level opponent fight on unfamiliar terms. For example, a specialist might insist on a kick-only contest if that removes the opponent's strongest options. This does not make the two fighters equal in overall ability.

**Working proposal**

Even a legendary martial artist can be vulnerable to exhaustion, poison, surprise, weapons, numbers, age, injury, or circumstances outside their expertise. Skill matters, but it does not make someone invincible or competent at everything.

## 11. Shared-world continuity

**Working proposal**

It may be useful to distinguish three layers:

- **World foundations / canon rules:** the shared facts and rules that every canon story should respect.
- **Official stories:** stories recognised as taking place in the world's continuity.
- **Unofficial or fan works:** stories using the setting but not automatically changing official continuity.

This structure remains open for discussion.
